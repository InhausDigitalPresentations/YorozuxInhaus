The scent code

Two ceremonies. One wood.

Chef Akira asks guests to arrive without scent, so nothing stands between them and the fish. In Abu Dhabi, scent is hospitality itself. Said as a rule, it clashes with local custom.

Yet the agarwood burned in Japanese kōdō is the same wood that gives the Gulf its oud. Both cultures hold it sacred. And in the Gulf, oud traditionally closes a gathering.

Arrive without scent. Leave with one.

Inside, the chef's ceremony. At the farewell, the Emirati one: oud offered at the door.

INHAUS proposes, to Yorozu and its PR agency

Tell the scent code as an encounter of two cultures, before anyone calls it a ban. Arabic written natively and reviewed by an Emirati voice.
